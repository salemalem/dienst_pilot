# Model card: C5 Cover Acceptance Ranker (Vertretungsvorschlag)

Status: built and evaluated on synthetic data. Nothing here describes a real department, and no number below has been measured on real staff or real cover searches.

## Intended use

Order the colleagues the validator (C2) says may take a shift, when a sick call or a gap needs cover. The cascade asks them one at a time with a 15-minute window; the Planner sees the same order for a gap (W7), for an escalation (W7b) and in the cover view. The order is a suggestion: C5 never assigns anything, and a cover still passes C2 and, unless the Planner's policy auto-approves it, the Planner.

The ordering has two parts that are kept apart (ADR-014):

1. **Policy.** People are bucketed by cover burden, the number of covers they took in the last 90 days, in buckets two covers wide (0 to 1, 2 to 3, ...). The least burdened bucket is asked first.
2. **Model.** P(accept) orders people only inside a bucket. A probability can never move a person across a bucket boundary (a property test). Ties fall back to lower hours against contract, then to the staff id.

## Out-of-scope uses

- Showing P(accept) to anybody. It never leaves the ranker: the port returns an ordered list, no API response carries it, and a privacy test fails if a key such as `p_accept` appears in any GET response for any persona. Staff see neither their own nor anyone's.
- Predicting an individual's behaviour for any purpose other than the order of a cover search; judging, scoring or comparing people.
- Using it as evidence about reliability or willingness of a named person. The model has no person identifier as an input.
- Other departments or real data.

## Data

- Synthetic: `data/synthetic/v1`, fictional department (`synthdata/ASSUMPTIONS.md`). The files read, with a SHA-256 each, are recorded in `eval/results/c5.json`; training re-checks each against the manifest. The generator's ground truth is never read by training (a test fails if code under `ml/` refers to it); only `eval/` reads it, for the replay and for the signal check.
- One row per cover offer (a colleague asked to take one shift): about 1,200 in 2021 to 2024 (training and selection), 183 in 2025 (held-out test). Label: accepted, against declined or no response. A cascade stops at the first accept and the planner asked people in a non-random order, so the rows are not a random sample of colleagues: the model estimates P(accept | asked), not P(accept) of someone nobody asked.
- Staff identifiers live only in memory during training; the bundle holds model parameters and no identifier (tests inspect it).

## Features (`ml/src/dienstpilot_ml/features/cover.py`, shared by training, eval and serving)

Hours against contract this month (credited hours over weekly hours / 5 per weekday), days since last cover, working shifts in the 7 days before the shift, notice period (request to shift start, 0 to 72 hours), shift type, weekday, time of the request, and the person's own confirmed preference for that shift type in the last 365 days (their `wish_work` entries minus their `avoid_shift` entries for it). Only preferences the person entered or confirmed are used, never one C4 inferred. In the generated history there are `wish_work` entries but no `avoid_shift` entries (those exist only in the product), so in training that feature is zero or positive; the product can also produce negative values, which the models never saw.

Excluded: age, gender, nationality, family status, absence history and any person identifier. Training never reads the absence table.

## Models and procedure

- B1 base rate; B2 logistic regression (L2, fixed strength); learner: LightGBM with isotonic calibration (pool adjacent violators, fitted on out-of-fold predictions of the rolling-origin folds, never on 2025). Hyperparameters from a fixed eight-point grid by cross-validated log-loss. Isotonic and logistic regression are implemented in numpy and scipy in this repository; no dependency was added (ADR-014).
- Rolling-origin cross-validation by year (2022, 2023, 2024) picks the best baseline; nothing is chosen on 2025.
- Promotion: the rule in `ml/cover_ranker/PROMOTION_RULE.md` was committed before any model was fitted. The learner is promoted only if on 2025 it beats the best baseline's Brier score by at least 2 % with a bootstrap interval (over cascades) that excludes zero, has a higher AUC (at least 0.55), and a calibration error of at most 0.10. Otherwise the best baseline stays champion, and every surface says so.
- Tracking: local MLflow (`make train`), registered model `c5-cover-ranker`; the API serves a committed bundle and never imports MLflow or SciPy.

Reading the tables (this run; the generated tables above are authoritative):

- **The learner was not promoted and the base rate is served.** On the 2025 offers the LightGBM model did not beat the best baseline on any of the four checks. With about 1,200 training offers, features that describe the situation but not the person, and a held-out year of 183 offers, there was no reliable signal to find. The generator's own acceptance probability has an AUC of about 0.70 on the same offers, but it depends on who the person is (a hidden willingness and load sensitivity), which the model is not allowed to see (no person identifier is an input).
- **Logistic regression scored better on 2025 than the base rate** (Brier and AUC), but it is not the model the rule puts to the test: the rule judges the learner against the best baseline chosen on cross-validation, which was the base rate, and the logistic model did worse than the base rate on cross-validation. We did not change the rule after seeing this. It is a pointer to what a later model could examine, not a result.
- **What the served ordering therefore is.** With a base-rate champion every candidate has the same probability, so inside a burden bucket the order follows hours against contract. The fairness-first policy with the champion is then a coarser version of the previous stand-in. In the replay it is not distinguishable from the stand-in on time to fill, cover-burden Gini, escalation or people asked (all paired intervals include zero), and the exact-burden sensitivity check (a-exact) is the stand-in ordering exactly, by construction. The buckets, fixed before any run, trade a little burden resolution for room that a model with real signal would use.
- **Most reliable first** closes gaps faster and asks fewer people, but it loads the same people: the cover-burden Gini is higher by 0.085 on average (the interval excludes zero). That is the trade-off the policy exists to prevent.
- **The absolute replay numbers are not comparable with the manual-era data.** The replay asks only people C2 allows (the manual era sometimes asked unchecked people), gives each person a 15-minute window and stops after six. About half of the simulated cascades therefore end with nobody accepting; in the historical data about a quarter ended without a cover from the asked colleagues.

## Limitations

- Synthetic data and a simulated replay: acceptance in the replay comes from the generator's behaviour model (hidden per-person willingness, a load term, a shift-liking term), read from the ground truth inside `eval/` only. Policies are compared on the same random draws, but the numbers say nothing about real people or real cascades.
- The replay's candidate pool is a re-implementation in `eval/` of the application's quick filter followed by the same validator (C2) call the product makes; it is not byte-tested against the application's code. The product's own tests cover the application side.
- Small held-out set: 183 offers in 126 cascades. Intervals are wide, and promotion on one year is a noisy decision.
- Selection effects: offers exist only for people the planner chose to ask, in an order that favoured the planner's favourites. P(accept | asked) is not P(accept).
- A response after the 15-minute window counts as no response in the replay; the generator draws delays of 2 to 19 minutes.
- Feature definitions are simplifications (hours against contract ignores leave credit and holidays; "confirmed preference" counts entries by shift type, not by weekday).
- The Situation C walkthrough (Dr. Weber) still puts Dr. Alvarez first with the base-rate champion, but only through the tie-break inside burden bucket 0 (she has the lowest hours against contract of the two people in it); she is not first because a model scored her highest. A champion with real signal could reorder that bucket, and nothing was tuned to prevent it.

## Fairness and privacy considerations

The policy exists for fairness: people who covered recently are asked later, whatever a model says. No protected attribute is an input and no person identifier is stored. The probability is not exposed to anyone, so it cannot become a way of labelling people as "reliable" or "unreliable". A person who often declines is not penalised by C5 in the order except through burden: the model has no memory of who they are. One consequence to watch in any later model with person-level signal: ordering within a bucket by willingness would ask the same willing people first inside their bucket; the bucket width, not the model, limits that.

## How to reproduce

```bash
make seed        # generates data/synthetic/v1 if missing
make train       # CV, fits, logs and registers the candidates in ml/mlflow (C3, C4 and C5)
make eval        # scores 2025 once, applies the rule, replays the sick calls, writes eval/results/c5.json
```
