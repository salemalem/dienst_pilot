# Model card: C6 Leave Feasibility Surrogate (Urlaubs-Schnellprüfung)

Status: built and evaluated on synthetic data. Nothing here describes a real department, and no number below has been measured on real staff or real leave requests.

## Intended use

Give a physician an instant hint, before they submit, whether a leave request is likely coverable. The hint is advisory. The incremental solver check (C1 with C2, `check_leave`) answers within seconds; the screen always shows the solver's answer and the buttons follow only that answer (ADR-015). Every disagreement between the hint and the solver is logged as an audit event without a name or a reason. The Planner's inbox (W6) shows the same hint next to the computed impact of a pending request.

The hint has three values: `coverable` (the model says yes), `not_coverable` (the score is under 0.10) and `tight` (in between, an abstention).

## Out-of-scope uses

- Deciding anything. A request is approved or declined by the Planner, and the roster by C1 and C2. The hint never gates the "request leave" button.
- Showing a probability to anybody. The port returns a three-valued hint; no API response carries a score.
- Judging a person's leave behaviour or deserving. The model has no person identifier as an input and was trained on simulated requests, not on what people asked for.
- Requests that span two months. They were never labelled (the solver answers `not_applicable`), so the model does not give a hint for them: the previous rule does, and the hint is marked as coming from the rule.
- Other departments or real data.

## What it learns

The label is the answer of the product's own M5 check, computed by the same function that the check job runs (`dienstpilot_solver.coverability.decide`, ADR-015), on simulated requests against the synthetic department:

- **coverable**: a valid roster exists for the month with the leave, and covering it does not burden colleagues beyond the thresholds (overtime up by more than 8 hours, or the highest weighted burden up by more than 2 points; `synthdata/ASSUMPTIONS.md` §18);
- **coverable with a trade-off**: coverable, but burdening, and one extra weekend by the requester in the month before compensates;
- **not coverable**: no valid roster exists with the leave.

The target is binary or three-class by a rule fixed before the first label (`ml/leave_surrogate/PROMOTION_RULE.md`): binary (1 = the solver says plain coverable) if the trade-off class is under 5 % of the training rows or under 100 rows. The generated tables below say which applied. A false yes, a hint of coverable where the solver says a trade-off or no, is the costly error, so the primary metric is the precision of a yes.

## Data

- Synthetic: `data/synthetic/v1`, fictional department (`synthdata/ASSUMPTIONS.md`). The labels are generated by `eval/run_c6_labels.py` (`make labels`, about an hour on an 11-core laptop, resumable and deterministic) and committed in `ml/leave_surrogate/labels/` with their hashes in `meta.json`. They hold request facts and counts, no staff identifier (a test checks). The generator's ground truth is not read at all.
- **The state a request is drawn against** is the department's leave as it stood at the moment of the request: every request of the dataset submitted before then, except those already declined, with approved and still-pending requests alike counted as leave (the product's cautious reading). No wishes, no unavailability and no worked days of the previous month exist 60 or more days ahead, so none enter. The year-to-date burden ledger is taken as of the start of the month (a simplification).
- **Two kinds of state, half each.** *Natural*: the state as it was. *Stress*: one to ten extra colleagues put on leave over the whole window, half of them as approved and half as pending leave: a counterfactual pile-up. The real planner stops approving at six concurrent people, so natural states almost never contain a "no". Without the stress states there would be nothing to learn and nothing to compare. They are simulated and are labelled as such everywhere, and the results lead with the natural states.
- Training and model selection use 2021 to 2024 (about 3,000 requests); 2025 (about 1,000 requests) is the held-out test, scored once.
- Labels are solved with the product's settings except two solver workers (so five can run at once); a random 200 are re-solved with the exact settings and the disagreement is reported below.

## Features (`ml/src/dienstpilot_ml/features/leave.py`, shared by training, eval and serving)

For the request window, with the requester counted as absent: minimum and mean daily coverage slack (people free for a shift minus the working headcount), qualification slack (minimum over the days, per qualification the demand names), colleagues on approved and on pending leave (maximum and mean per day), the number of distinct overlapping colleagues, public holidays, school-holiday days and weekend days in the window, its length, the notice in days, the requester's role and their qualifications, and the month of the year.

Excluded: any person identifier, age, gender, nationality, family status, a person's leave history, reasons and sickness.

## Models and procedure

- B1 the previous stand-in rule: a yes if at most four colleagues are on approved leave on any day of the window (it ignores pending requests, as it always did). B2 logistic regression (L2, fixed strength). Learner: LightGBM, hyperparameters from a fixed eight-point grid by cross-validated log-loss.
- **Operating point.** Precision between classifiers is only comparable at the same recall, because any model raises precision by saying yes less often. For B2 and the learner the threshold is the one that maximises precision subject to out-of-fold recall at least B1's out-of-fold recall, chosen on 2022 to 2024 and frozen.
- Rolling-origin cross-validation by year (test years 2022, 2023, 2024) picks the best baseline; nothing is chosen on 2025.
- Promotion (`PROMOTION_RULE.md`, committed before any label or model): on the pooled 2025 mix the learner's precision of a yes must be higher than the best baseline's by at least one point with a bootstrap interval (resampling months, then requests) that excludes zero; its recall must be at least the stand-in's; and the full hint path must run in under 50 ms at the 95th percentile. Otherwise the best baseline stays champion and every surface says so.
- Tracking: local MLflow (`make train`), registered model `c6-leave-surrogate`; the API serves a committed bundle and never imports MLflow or SciPy.

Reading the tables (this run; the generated tables above are authoritative):

- **The learner was not promoted; the logistic regression is served.** The learner failed two of the three checks on the pooled 2025 mix. Its precision of a yes is higher than the best baseline's by about one point, but the bootstrap interval includes zero. Its recall is lower than the previous rule's, so it fails the recall check. Cross-validation had already picked the logistic regression as the best baseline, and the rule judges the learner against that, so the baseline stays in service. We did not change the rule after seeing this. One thing the rule could not know in advance: the best baseline's precision is already near the ceiling, so a margin of one point leaves almost no room for any learner.
- **On realistic states, nothing separates the methods much.** In the natural states 97 to 99 % of the labels are plain "coverable" (see the label table) and every method says yes almost always. The precision of a yes is about the same for the previous rule and the logistic regression, and the errors of every method there are requests the solver answers with a trade-off (it sees the burden of the weighted year-to-date ledger, which the hint's features do not). The logistic regression says yes to more of the plain-coverable requests (higher recall).
- **The difference shows in the simulated stress states.** There the previous rule's false yes are mostly requests the solver cannot cover at all (it counts approved leave only, and the stress states put half of the extra leave in as pending requests); the served model has almost none, at the cost of saying yes less often (lower recall in the stress states). That is the trade the pre-registered rule makes: a false yes is the costly error. "Including simulated stress states", the served model's precision of a yes is higher than the previous rule's by several points, with a recall a little lower than the previous rule's on 2025 (the recall constraint held on the 2022 to 2024 out-of-fold predictions, not on 2025). The sensitivity row shows that a previous rule that also counted pending requests would close part of the gap in the stress states; it was not eligible and was not part of the rule.
- **The hint is conservative and often abstains in the stress states.** A "tight" hint (the middle band) is an abstention and is never counted as a disagreement. Agreement with the solver is reported with the counts of agreeing, disagreeing and abstaining hints; the disagreements in 2025 are few, and most are hints of "coverable" where the solver found a trade-off.
- **Situation A is untouched.** Dr. Kaya's request for 14 to 27 July 2027 is answered by the solver as it always was (coverable, no trade-off, two pending colleagues counted). The served hint for it is "coverable", so there is no disagreement to show there; a test replaces the hint with a wrong one and checks that the solver's answer is what the screen gets and that the disagreement is logged. Nothing was tuned to make hint and solver agree.
- **Latency.** The full hint path (facts from the department's state, features, prediction) takes well under a millisecond at the 95th percentile in this process; the endpoint end to end takes a few milliseconds more. Both are wall-clock on one machine.
- **The 2025 labels differ from 2021 to 2024 in one way worth noting:** the share of plain-coverable requests that the solver turns into a trade-off is higher in 2025's natural states than in the training years, a shift whose cause was not investigated. It is the main source of the false yes in the natural states.


## Limitations

- Synthetic data and simulated requests. The labels are the solver's answers on synthetic rosters, not what a real planner would have decided. The stress states are counterfactual, and precision depends on their share, which was fixed at 50 % before any run and was not tuned.
- The natural states almost never contain a "no", so on them every method looks good and there is little to tell them apart; the stress states are what separates them, and they are made up.
- The year-to-date burden ledger is taken as of the start of the month, and the previous month's worked days are not part of the state, because at the time of a request they do not exist. The product has the same gap, so this is consistent, but it is a simplification of what the solver sees later.
- A request over two months, or a month that is already published, gets no model hint (and no label).
- The label depends on the solver's time limit: a month the solver cannot solve in its short check budget is labelled "not coverable". The fidelity check above counts how often a longer run would have changed the label in a random sample.
- Latency is wall-clock on one machine and is not reproducible; it is not the latency of any real deployment.

## Fairness and privacy considerations

No protected attribute and no person identifier is an input, and the bundle holds trees, thresholds and the feature list only (tests inspect it). The hint concerns the request window, not the person: the requester's role and qualifications enter because the solver's answer depends on them, and the role is not a protected attribute. Staff see no colleague names on M5, and the disagreement log carries no name and no reason (the check id joins to the requester inside the database only). A hint of `not_coverable` does not stop anyone from asking: the solver and the Planner decide.

## How to reproduce

```bash
make seed        # generates data/synthetic/v1 if missing
make labels      # solver runs on simulated requests (about an hour); the committed labels make this optional
make train       # CV, fits, logs and registers the candidates in ml/mlflow (C3 to C6)
make eval        # scores 2025 once, applies the rule, writes eval/results/c6.json
```
