# DienstPilot

**KI-gestützte Dienstplanung für Kliniken / AI-assisted duty scheduling for hospitals**

Process description: how the software is used in daily hospital work.
Prepared as a discussion document. Non-technical by design: it describes what each person does, what the system does, and who has to approve what.

Version 0.1, 10 August 2026

> This document describes the intended product behaviour and is the functional specification for the prototype. Technical design: `ARCHITECTURE.md`. Screens and copy: `UI_SPEC.md`.

---

## 1. Who works with the system

The software mirrors the hierarchy that already exists in the hospital. Nobody receives a power they do not have today, and nobody loses one.

| Role in the clinic | In the app | What this person does in DienstPilot |
|---|---|---|
| Assistenzarzt / junior and assistant doctors | Mitarbeiter (Staff) | Enters availability and preferences, requests leave, offers or accepts shift swaps, reports sickness, accepts an offered replacement shift. Sees own roster, own hours, own fairness balance. |
| Fachärztin / Oberarzt (specialist, senior physician) | Mitarbeiter+ (Staff, extended) | Everything above, plus visibility of the full departmental roster and the qualification coverage of each shift. May be given delegated approval rights for same-day cover. |
| Oberarzt / Chefarzt responsible for the roster | Planer (Planner) | Opens and closes the request window, reviews the AI proposals, adjusts, approves and publishes the roster. Decides every request that the system escalates. |
| Klinikleitung / Personalabteilung / Betriebsrat | Aufsicht (Oversight) | Read-only. Sees compliance reports, overtime, cost, fairness statistics and the complete audit trail. Cannot change a single shift. |
| IT / Administration | Administrator | Sets up departments, contracts, qualifications, shift types and rules once. No involvement in daily operation. |

**The key sentence for the department meeting: the AI never approves anything. It only ever proposes.**

## 2. Who approves what

Every action in the system has exactly one owner and one approver. This is the part that protects the responsible physician, and it should be shown early to anyone who fears a loss of control.

| Action | Requested by | Approved by |
|---|---|---|
| Availability and shift preferences | Each doctor | No approval needed. Treated as input, never as a promise. |
| Leave / holiday request | Each doctor | Planner. The system shows immediately whether the request is coverable and what it would cost the roster. |
| Monthly roster | Generated by the system | Planner. Not visible to anyone until approved and published. |
| Shift swap between two doctors | Doctor A, accepted by Doctor B | Automatic if qualification, rest periods and hours all stay valid; otherwise escalated to the Planner. |
| Sick report | The sick doctor | No approval. Takes effect immediately and triggers the replacement search. |
| Replacement for a sick colleague | Proposed by the system, accepted by the covering doctor | Automatic within the pre-set rules of the Planner; escalated when it creates overtime, uses an external locum, or crosses departments. |
| Change to a published roster | Planner | Planner, with a mandatory reason; all affected doctors are notified. |
| Rules, contracts, qualifications | Administrator | Clinic management |

## 3. The full process, month by month

This is the cycle the department repeats every month. Today it takes a senior physician the better part of a weekend. With DienstPilot it is a sequence of short, defined steps.

### Step 1: Setup, once only, at the beginning

The administrator enters the framework of the department: shift types (early, late, night, on-call, weekend), how many doctors each shift requires and with which qualification, contract hours per person, and the legal rules: rest periods, maximum consecutive nights, maximum weekend frequency. This happens one time. Afterwards it is only touched when something structurally changes.

### Step 2: The request window opens

Six weeks before the month begins, the planner opens the window with one click. Every doctor receives a notification on the phone.

Each doctor now enters, in about two minutes: which days they cannot work, which days they would prefer to work, leave requests, and preferences such as „rather nights than weekends“. They can also mark one or two wishes as important, which tells the system where to prioritise when not everything can be satisfied.

The planner sees a simple progress bar: who has responded and who has not. No more chasing people in the corridor, no more preferences arriving by WhatsApp, e-mail and sticky note.

### Step 3: The system builds the roster

When the window closes, the system generates the roster. It respects all hard rules absolutely (staffing levels, qualifications, rest periods, working-time law, contract hours) and then optimises what remains: as many fulfilled wishes as possible, an even distribution of nights, weekends and holidays, and the lowest possible overtime.

It does not produce one plan. It produces two or three complete, valid variants, each with a plain-language summary, for example: „Variant A: 91% of wishes granted, night shifts evenly balanced, 12 hours of overtime. Variant B: 84% of wishes granted, zero overtime, Dr. Weber receives one weekend more than the average.“ The system marks its recommendation and explains in one sentence why.

### Step 4: The planner reviews and approves

The responsible physician compares the variants side by side, and can change anything by dragging a shift. If a change breaks a rule, the system says so immediately and in plain words („rest period of 11 hours not met“) and offers a correction. If a change creates a gap, it suggests who could fill it.

Typical time required: fifteen to thirty minutes instead of a weekend. When satisfied, the planner presses **Approve and publish**. Only at that moment does the roster become visible to the department.

### Step 5: Publication

Every doctor immediately has the roster in the app and, if wanted, synchronised into their private calendar. Rejected leave requests are shown with the reason. Each person can see their own fairness balance: nights, weekends and holidays worked this year compared with the departmental average. This transparency removes most of the arguments before they start, because the objection is rarely the shift itself. It is the suspicion of being asked more often than the others.

### Step 6: Daily operation during the month

Swaps, short-term absences and sick reports are handled inside the app, described in the scenarios in the next section. The planner is involved only where a real decision is required.

### Step 7: End of month

As soon as the month is over, the system prepares the documentation by itself: hours worked per person, overtime, rest-period compliance, night and weekend distribution, and the complete decision trail. The planner reads it and confirms it with one click; that locks the month and releases the payroll export. Only then does the documentation go to payroll and, on request, to the works council. What used to be a separate manual task shrinks to one confirmation.

## 4. Three situations, told the way they actually happen

These are the moments that decide whether the software is loved or ignored. They are worth walking through in detail with the department.

### Situation A: A junior doctor wants two weeks off in July

Dr. Kaya opens the app and selects 14–27 July. Before she even submits, the system shows her: two other colleagues have already requested overlapping days, and her request is still coverable, without overtime and without raising the highest burden in the department.

The planner sees the request with everything already calculated: the effect on staffing, who would cover, whether it creates overtime, and how much leave Dr. Kaya has taken relative to her colleagues this year. He approves with one tap. Total time spent by the senior physician: under a minute. Dr. Kaya has an answer the same day instead of waiting three weeks for the next planning round.

Had covering her leave pushed colleagues into overtime or above that burden, the system would have offered her a trade-off within the rules, for example one extra weekend duty in June, which she could accept in the same screen or decline.

### Situation B: Two doctors exchange a shift

Dr. Mehta cannot work his early shift on Saturday, 21 November. Giving it away alone would leave him below his contracted hours, so he offers it as an exchange. The system does not broadcast it to everyone; it shows the offer only to colleagues who are qualified for that shift, legally able to take it, and able to give a shift back without breaking a rule, which is exactly the check that today has to be done in someone's head.

Dr. Lorenz takes his Saturday shift and gives him her early shift on Friday, 27 November, in return. The system re-verifies both rosters. Everything remains valid, so the exchange is confirmed automatically, both rosters update, and the planner receives an information notice, not a request. If, however, the exchange had pushed Dr. Lorenz over her hours or broken a rest period, the system would have held it and sent it to the planner for a decision, with the reason stated.

### Situation C: A doctor calls in sick at 06:40

Dr. Weber reports sick in the app. Two minutes later:

- The system has identified everyone who could cover the shift: qualified, off-duty, legally permitted, not over hours.
- It has ranked them by who has covered least recently, so the same willing colleague is not burned out.
- It has sent the request to the top candidate with a deadline of fifteen minutes, then automatically to the next.

Dr. Alvarez accepts on her phone. The roster updates for everyone. The planner receives a message that the gap is closed.

The senior physician made no phone calls and lost no clinical time. If nobody accepts, or if the only remaining option means overtime or an external locum, then and only then does the system escalate, and it presents the planner with the ranked options and the consequence of each, rather than an empty problem.

## 5. What changes for each person

| | Today | With DienstPilot |
|---|---|---|
| Junior / assistant doctor | Sends wishes by e-mail or in person, never knows if they were seen, learns the roster late, argues about fairness | Enters wishes in two minutes, sees the status of every request, has the roster in the phone calendar, sees own fairness balance |
| Senior physician responsible for the roster | Spends a weekend a month building the plan, an afternoon of phone calls per sick call, carries every conflict personally | Reviews and approves in 20 minutes, is contacted only for genuine decisions, has every decision documented |
| Clinic management | No reliable view of overtime, compliance or cost until it becomes a problem | Live overview, automatic compliance documentation, planning cost visible in advance |
| The department as a whole | Roster is a recurring source of friction and a known reason for staff leaving | Distribution is visible and demonstrably fair; the discussion moves from suspicion to facts |

## 6. What the system will never do

Stated explicitly, because this is what a clinic will ask about first.

- It never publishes a roster that a responsible physician has not approved.
- It never breaks a legal rule. Rest periods, maximum hours and night-shift limits are hard constraints, not preferences to be optimised away.
- It never orders a doctor to take a shift. It asks, and the doctor accepts or declines.
- It never hides its reasoning. Every proposal comes with a plain-language explanation, and every decision is recorded: who requested, what was proposed, who approved, when.
- It never replaces the planner. It removes the mechanical work and leaves the judgement where it belongs.

## 7. How it is delivered

- **Mobile app for all doctors.** Preferences, leave requests, the roster, swaps, sick reports, accepting cover. Designed to be usable in under two minutes, one-handed, between patients.
- **Web dashboard for the planner and management.** Roster generation and review, approvals, rules, reports, audit trail. Used on a real screen because that is where planning is actually done.
- **Nothing to install in the hospital (the design intent).** No IT project on their side; data to be hosted in Germany and handled under GDPR principles such as data minimisation, with exports so the clinic is not locked in. None of this is certified, reviewed or deployed: the prototype runs locally on synthetic data, and the payroll and audit exports are the only exports built so far.

### Suggested first step

One department, one month, running in parallel with the existing method. The department keeps its old roster as a safety net; the system produces its proposal alongside. At the end of the month the two are compared: hours saved, wishes granted, rules broken. That comparison is the business case, and it costs the hospital nothing but a little patience.
