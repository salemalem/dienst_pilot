# ADR-015: C6 leave surrogate, labels from the product's own check, stress states, and a hint that never decides

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-10-22
- Phase: 8d (C6 leave feasibility surrogate)
- Builds on: ADR-009, ADR-012, ADR-013, ADR-014

## Context

M5 shows an instant hint and then the answer of the incremental solver check (C1 with C2). The hint was a stand-in: approved leave per day against the concurrency limit. C6 is meant to replace it with a model trained on what the solver actually says. Three things shape the design. The label is the solver's answer, so it should come from exactly the code the product runs. Real department states almost never contain a "no" (the planner stops approving at six concurrent), so a model and a rule cannot be told apart on them. And a hint that is wrong is cheap only as long as it never decides anything.

## Decision

1. **Same convention as C3 to C5, and a new first step.** `ml/leave_surrogate/PROMOTION_RULE.md` was committed before any label was generated and before any model was fitted. Its hash is recorded in `eval/results/c6.json` and a test fails if the file changes afterwards. The rule fixes the request simulation, the two kinds of state, the target rule, the features, the operating point, the promotion checks, and the reporting order.
2. **One definition of the answer.** The decision logic of `check_leave` moved into a pure function, `dienstpilot_solver.coverability.decide`, with the solver, validator and state passed in as callbacks. The job and the label generator call the same function. It solves the leave first, because an infeasible answer does not need the baseline; the outputs of the job did not change (the existing trade-off and Situation A tests pass unchanged).
3. **Labels from simulated requests, with stress states.** A request is a Monday-aligned block of 5, 10 or 15 working days inside one month, submitted 60 to 150 days ahead, against the leave as it stood then (approved and pending counted as leave). Half the requests use the state as it was (*natural*). Half have one to ten extra colleagues put on leave over the window, half of them approved and half pending (*stress*, counterfactual). The 50/50 split was fixed in advance and not tuned. Reports lead with the natural states, then the stress states, then the pooled mix, and wherever the pooled figure appears it is labelled "including simulated stress states" (in `c6.json`, the model card, W13 and the engine note).
4. **Binary target, by a rule fixed before the pilot.** Labels keep three classes. If coverable-with-a-trade-off is under 5 % of the training rows or under 100 rows, the target is binary: 1 only if the solver says plain coverable. The training labels have 28 such rows (0.9 %), so the target is binary. A false yes is then a hint of "coverable" where the solver says a trade-off or no.
5. **Precision at pinned recall.** Precision between classifiers is only comparable at the same recall, so the logistic regression and the learner get the threshold with the highest out-of-fold precision subject to out-of-fold recall at least the previous rule's, chosen on 2022 to 2024 and frozen. Promotion needs, on 2025, a precision of a yes at least one point above the best baseline's with a bootstrap interval that excludes zero, a recall at least the previous rule's, and a hint latency p95 under 50 ms on the full path (features and prediction; the endpoint's time is reported, not gated).
6. **The hint is advisory and the solver's answer is shown.** M5 shows the solver's answer, and the buttons follow only that. If the hint differed, one line says so. Every disagreement (a hint of coverable against a trade-off or not coverable, or not coverable against coverable; "tight" abstains; `not_applicable` is not scored) is stored with the check (`hint_outcome`, `hint_version`, `hint_basis`, `agreement`, an expand-only migration) and written as an audit event `leave.hint_disagreed` without a name or a reason (the check id joins to the requester inside the database only). Situation A is untouched: the solver's answer is what is shown, and a test replaces the hint with a wrong one to prove it.
7. **The port carries the situation.** `LeaveFeasibility.hint(facts, limit)` takes `LeaveFacts`, the shared definition of `dienstpilot_ml.features.leave`, built from the database by `app/leave_hint.py` and by the label generator from the Parquet files, so training and serving cannot differ. A request over two months was never labelled and gets the previous rule, marked `basis: "rule"`.
8. **The stand-in is removed from the registry.** The previous rule survives as B1 inside `ml/` (it is also the fallback for requests over two months). `C6LeaveFeasibility` loads only the committed bundle (`ml/leave_surrogate/artifacts/champion/`); serving needs numpy and LightGBM, and an import-linter contract covers `dienstpilot_ml.leave_surrogate`.
9. **Label generation is fast, resumable and checked.** About 4,000 requests take about an hour on an 11-core laptop in five processes, with two solver workers each (`make labels`, under `caffeinate` on macOS); labels are cached by request id and committed with their hashes. A random 200 are re-solved with the product's exact settings (eight workers) and the share that differs is reported. CI re-solves a few committed sample labels.
10. **No new dependencies.** numpy, scipy, LightGBM, polars and OR-Tools are already in the stack.

## Consequences

- The learner did not pass the rule on the first and only run: its precision gain over the best baseline is about one point with an interval that includes zero, and its recall is lower than the previous rule's. The logistic regression, chosen by cross-validation as the best baseline, is served. This is the same outcome as C3 to C5 and is reported as such.
- On natural states the methods are barely distinguishable, and the stress states, which are simulated, are what show the difference. The model card says so and the figures lead with the natural states.
- The recall constraint held on the 2022 to 2024 out-of-fold predictions but not on 2025, where the served model's recall is a little below the previous rule's. The rule judged the learner on it, not the baseline; we report it rather than rewording it.
- One label out of 200 depends on the number of solver workers (the exact-settings re-solve says trade-off where the two-worker run said coverable): labels near the overtime threshold are not perfectly stable.
- The year-to-date burden ledger is taken as of the start of the month and the previous month's worked days are not part of a simulated state. The solver sees the same gap in the product at the time of a request, so this is consistent but a simplification.
- Disagreements are visible only in the audit trail and in the counts of `c6.json`; they are not training data yet (a retraining loop is not built).
